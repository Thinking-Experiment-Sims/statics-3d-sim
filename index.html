📖 3D Statics Lab Guide & Reference AP Physics C • Mechanics

📐 1. Measuring Elevation Angles with the Protractor

In this lab, elevation angles (θelev) are not given automatically. Students must measure them using the on-screen protractor:

1 Click an elevation camera preset above the 3D viewport: 📐 Elev C1, 📐 Elev C2, or 📐 Elev C3. This aligns the camera perpendicular to that cable's vertical plane with 0° perspective distortion.
2 Observe the reference triangle: the horizontal dashed line represents the 0° horizontal baseline, and the vertical plumb drop marks the right angle.
3 Click 📐 Snap Protractor to Knot. Drag the rotating pointer arm until it aligns with the cable line of action up to the anchor ring.
4 Read the angle off the dual-scale protractor dial and enter it into the θelev input in the 3D Component Table.

📏 2. Reading Vernier Spring Scales

Each suspension cable is coupled to a standard classroom 0–10 N tubular spring scale:

1 Scale Graduation: Major tick marks represent 1.0 N; intermediate minor ticks represent 0.2 N. Students should interpolate to the nearest 0.05 N.
2 Ideal Scenario: Cables are inextensible and digital readouts display exact theoretical values.
3 Real Lab Mode: Scale springs stretch under Hooke's Law (k = 250 N/m), causing realistic knot sag and simulated reading uncertainty (±0.05 N to ±0.10 N).

🧮 3. 3D Vector Statics Mathematics

Because the three cables are non-coplanar, each tension vector &vec;Ti resolves along three Cartesian axes:

&vec;ui = (&vec;rclamp,i - &vec;rknot) / |&vec;rclamp,i - &vec;rknot|
Tix = Ti • uix  |  Tiy = Ti • sin(θelev,i)  |  Tiz = Ti • uiz

For the suspended knot in static equilibrium (Σ&vec;F = 0):

ΣFx = T1x + T2x + T3x = 0
ΣFy = T1y + T2y + T3y - m•g = 0 ⇒ m = [ Σ Ti sin(θelev,i) ] / g
ΣFz = T1z + T2z + T3z = 0

🧪 4. Solving the Mystery Mass Challenge

1 Switch to 🧪 2. Mystery Mass Challenge tab in the upper scenario strip. A mystery mass with unknown weight is suspended.
2 Read tensions T₁, T₂, T₃ from the three spring scales.
3 Switch into each elevation view (Elev C1, C2, C3) and measure each elevation angle θelev with the protractor.
4 Calculate vertical components Tiy = Ti • sin(θelev,i) and sum them to obtain Fg. Divide by g = 9.80 m/s² and multiply by 1000 to get grams.
5 Enter your computed mass in grams and click Check Mass.

📖 3D Statics Lab Guide & Reference Manual

📐 1. How to Measure Elevation Angles

Elevation angles are not printed on the apparatus to encourage genuine measurement with the virtual protractor:

1 Click one of the elevation camera presets: 📐 Elev C1, 📐 Elev C2, or 📐 Elev C3 located directly above the canvas or in the workbench tabs.
2 The camera locks into true perpendicular 2D projection against that cable's vertical plane with 0° perspective distortion.
3 The dashed colored line is the horizontal baseline (0°). The vertical plumb line forms a 90° right-angle at the floor.
4 Click 📐 Snap Protractor to Knot. Rotate the protractor arm to lay directly across the cable to the top anchor ring. Read θelev off the protractor scale.

📏 2. Reading Vernier Spring Scales

Each cable is suspended from an adjustable perimeter stand equipped with a 0–10 N tubular spring scale:

1 Each numbered graduation represents 1.0 N; intermediate tick marks represent 0.2 N.
2 In Real Lab Mode, springs obey Hooke's Law and exhibit realistic measurement uncertainty (±0.05 N to ±0.1 N).

🧮 3. 3D Statics Equilibrium Math

In 3D static equilibrium, the vector sum of all forces acting on the suspension knot must equal zero:

ΣFx = T1x + T2x + T3x = 0
ΣFy = T1•sin(θelev1) + T2•sin(θelev2) + T3•sin(θelev3) - mg = 0
ΣFz = T1z + T2z + T3z = 0

Thus, the suspended mass can be determined directly from the three measured tensions and their elevation angles:

m = [ T₁•sin(θelev1) + T₂•sin(θelev2) + T₃•sin(θelev3) ] / g
